# Decide hits its noise ceiling; robustness decides the rest

The frontier research says Decide is already close to state of the art for what it is: a 17M fine-tuned specialist that calibrates and abstains, running on CPU in single-digit milliseconds. Almost no paper-grade technique will add more than about one point on our own dev set, because the remaining in-domain headroom sits under label-noise ceilings. Route abstention (0.536) already matches the ceiling implied by 5–6% random flips. Approve's quality term (0.617) is capped by refusals driven by account history the ticket never shows. The four seeds we trained differ by about two points from luck alone. The real exposure is a different judge dataset. About 22 of our 85.2 points come from the grounded decision being perfect on template data: quality 1.0, Brier skill about 1.0, abstention 1.0. Published evidence shows a 17M Ettin grounding model falling from 90.9 F1 on its own synthetic data to 68.5 on real RAG outputs. Arithmetic on the judges' own scorer shows that 10% grounded errors on a new dataset would cost about 11 points if they are confidently wrong, but under 1 point if the model is honestly uncertain about them. So the highest-value work is not a bigger model or a cleverer calibrator. Four steps come first: (1) build a shifted-dev stress set; (2) make grounded and approve fail *uncertainly* rather than confidently, by softening the hard-coded −8 veto, adding hard-negative and paraphrase-positive data, and replacing fixed amount buckets with a parser plus a monotone rule; (3) fit calibration and thresholds on that shifted data; (4) re-score the checkpoints we already have (32M, 150M, the seed ensemble) on it. None of these needs extra inference latency. Against Jev and Laya, the defensible pitch is size, on-box latency, and an abstain signal that works. It is not accuracy, which has never been measured on a common benchmark.

## Our in-domain score sits within about one point of its ceiling

The judges' scorer (`selfcheck.py`, mirrored in [decide/scoring.py](../../decide/scoring.py)) makes the ceilings concrete. Quality is the mean of three skill scores on the *answered* set: route macro-F1 normalised against the majority class, approve cost mapped as `0.25 + 0.75·(hold − cost)/hold`, and grounded macro-F1. Each is scaled by `min(1, coverage/0.8)`. Calibration is `0.5·(1 − meanECE/0.20) + 0.5·meanBrierSkill`, where ECE uses 15 equal-width bins: top-label confidence for route, and `p_true` against the label for the binaries. Abstention is the mean over decisions of `1 − risk@80% / full error`. Cases are ranked by `max(p, 1−p)` and a binary counts as correct when `(p > 0.5) == y`, and **this ranking ignores our abstain flags entirely**. That last point settles an open question from the abstention notes. The scorer's abstention part depends only on how well the emitted probability ranks errors around 0.5. Our cost-aware approve rule (`min(3(1−p), p) − λp`, already shipped) therefore helps only the answered-set quality, not the abstention part.

Our shipped numbers on dev ([results.json](../../results.json)) show where each part stands:

| Part (weight) | Component values | What one unit of improvement buys |
|---|---|---|
| Quality 0.852 (40%) | route skill 0.938 (macro-F1 0.943, coverage 0.89); approve cost score 0.617 (cost 0.191 vs always-hold 0.374, coverage 0.85); grounded 1.0 | +0.01 route macro-F1 = **+0.15 pts**; −0.01 approve cost = **+0.27 pts**; each 0.01 of coverage below 0.80 on one decision = **−0.16 pts** |
| Calibration 0.846 (25%) | ECE route 0.035, approve 0.033, grounded 0.0002 (mean 0.023); mean Brier skill 0.806 | −0.01 mean ECE = **+0.63 pts**; +0.01 mean Brier skill = **+0.13 pts** |
| Latency 1.00 (20%) | p95 2.5 ms (Mac, HTTP), 4.3 ms (4 cores), 5.4–6.3 ms (2 cores) | already at full marks; 25 ms is the full-marks line |
| Abstention 0.667 (15%) | route 0.536 (risk@80 0.054 vs full error 0.117); approve 0.465 (0.062 vs 0.115); grounded 1.0 (no errors) | +0.01 on one decision = **+0.05 pts** |

Three ceilings follow from the data findings in [context.md](../team/context.md). **Route abstention is already at its floor.** If 5–6% of route labels are flipped at random, independently of the text, those flips spread evenly over confidence ranks, and the retained 80% keeps a 5–6% error rate whatever the ranking. The ceiling is therefore `1 − η/e ≈ 1 − 0.055/0.117 ≈ 0.49–0.57`, and we measure 0.536. The same bound falls out of the E-AURC framing, which compares a confidence ranking against the best ranking possible at a given error rate ([Geifman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08206)), and the ceiling drops further, toward `1 − 1.25η/e`, if flips land on cases the model finds easy. **Approve quality is aleatoric.** About 20% of under-cap requests are refused for account-history reasons the text never contains, so no text model can push its cost much lower. The literature agrees that only new observable features (segments, metadata) can rank such errors ([Franc, Prusa & Voracek, JMLR 2023](https://jmlr.org/papers/v24/21-0048.html) show that every reject formulation reduces to ranking by the Bayes posterior, which cannot see hidden fields). **Measurement noise exceeds most effects.** Our four seeds score 82.73 / 83.82 / 83.73 / 84.78 cross-fitted, with about 1.0 SD across dev halves ([ensemble_eval.txt](ensemble_eval.txt)). The shipped seed's lead is mostly luck, and a fresh seed should be expected to land near 83.8. ECE on 1,500 items has its own noise floor of about 0.01–0.02 for a perfectly calibrated model ([Roelofs et al., AISTATS 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.08668) on binned-ECE bias). Put together, the in-domain headroom left is roughly 0.3–0.8 points from ECE on route and approve, plus a few tenths from approve cost. Any idea claiming more on our own data should be distrusted until it beats the seed spread on the 6,500 out-of-fold predictions with bootstrap intervals.

The i.i.d. dev score overstates real-world performance. |

Three findings in this map deserve more than a table cell. First, **calibration alone is no differentiator.** Every zero-shot classifier on Metatext's typed-decisions harness reaches ECE between 0.016 and 0.093 after held-out temperature fitting, from GLiNER2 at 0.029 to GLiClass-large at 0.093 ([Metatext leaderboard](https://metatext.io/system-one/typed-decisions-benchmark)). Post-hoc temperature is a universal fix. The scarce property is a confidence that *ranks errors*, which is what our abstention part measures and what Laya's escalate head fails at (below). Second, **label smoothing and noisy-label methods are traps for this metric mix.** Label smoothing suppresses the max logit more on likely-correct samples than on errors, which degrades selective classification ([Xia et al., ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14715)). It also loses to MLE plus TS in-domain ([Desai & Durrett](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07892)). Pretrained BERT is already robust to random label noise, and noise-handling methods "do not always improve its performance, and may even deteriorate it" ([Zhu et al., 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.insights-1.8/)). Our post-hoc ε-floor gets the calibration benefit of smoothing without touching the logit ranking. Third, **the grounding literature's central result transfers directly.** MiniCheck-FT5 drops from 74.7 to 59.9 BAcc when both of its synthetic hard-negative sets are removed ([MiniCheck](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.10774)). FactCG's graph-edited negatives push the best 0.4B checker to 77.2 with tuned thresholds ([FactCG](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.17144)). In this area, data that deletes exactly the needed evidence matters more than model size. Numeric reasoning is a known blind spot of learned NLI: nine NLI models showed essentially zero gain over the majority baseline on quantitative entailment in [EQUATE](https://aclanthology.org/K19-1033/). That supports keeping our symbolic number normaliser rather than trusting the encoder with digits.

On the systems side, the "typed decision" framing is now an industry category, and our design matches its best practice. Jev exposes Choice/Score/Noul primitives, pins versioned model IDs, and defines calibration as a group-level property that does not guarantee any single answer ([TypeSafe docs](https://docs.typesafe.ai/concepts/system-one)). Laya scores each option at its own [MASK] token and recommends refitting one temperature per (question type, option count) ([Laya card](https://huggingface.co/convaiinnovations/laya)). Salesforce Einstein uses ordered thresholds for recommend, pre-select and auto-apply, falling back a tier when confidence is low ([Trailhead](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/en/content/learn/modules/service_case_class/turn-on-einstein-case-classification)). Stripe Radar uses a review band (default 65) below a block threshold (default 75) ([Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/radar/risk-settings.md)). Our act / abstain structure with a 3:1 Bayes threshold of 0.75 ([Elkan, IJCAI 2001](https://mlanthology.org/ijcai/2001/elkan2001ijcai-foundations)) is the same two-threshold pattern, and judges from industry will recognise it.

## A different dataset puts about 22 points at risk, mostly through grounded

The robustness question decides the competition, because the scorer turns *confident* errors into losses across all four parts at once. Grounded currently scores a perfect third in quality, Brier skill and abstention, worth about 13.3 + 4.2 + 5.0 ≈ 22.5 of our 85.2 points. Take an illustrative scenario where a new KB style drops grounded accuracy to 90%. If those errors sit at the probability bounds, as our −8 flag weights and near-saturated Platt map would produce, the scorer arithmetic gives roughly −2.2 (quality), −2.1 (ECE), −1.7 (Brier skill) and −5.0 (abstention, since confident errors are not ranked low): **about −11 points**. If the same errors instead come out near p ≈ 0.5, they fall below the abstain threshold and leave the answered-set F1 intact. Coverage stays above 0.8, ECE barely moves, and they fill the bottom 20% of the ranking: **about −0.5 points**. This is scorer arithmetic under an assumed error rate, not a forecast. It shows that *how* the model fails on new data matters an order of magnitude more than anything we can still gain in-domain.

The evidence that grounded will see errors is strong. TinyLettuce, built on our exact Ettin-17M backbone, scores 90.9 F1 on same-distribution synthetic data but 68.5 on RAGTruth ([TinyLettuce](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/KRLabsOrg/LettuceDetect@main/docs/TINYLETTUCE.md)). Our wrong drafts change exactly one slot, and our key rule ("no correct draft states a number+unit its KB lacks") is a property of the hackathon generator. The following is our inference, not a sourced result. Real support replies routinely contain correct numbers absent from the KB: the customer's own refund amount, an order ID, today's date, "step 2". Our learned flag weights start at −8 and add straight to the logit, so on such drafts the rule fires and produces exactly the confident false "unsupported" that the scenario above punishes. The same pattern recurs elsewhere. The normaliser hard-codes "a month = 30 days". The approve features include tier × amount-bucket one-hots fitted to this generator's caps (free ≈ $20–30, pro ≈ $100–120, enterprise ≈ $375–400). The route ε and bias b are fitted to this generator's flip rate and class priors. Unlabelled adaptation cannot recover changed caps: label-shift EM fixes class frequencies, not a new p(y|x) ([Alexandari et al., ICML 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.06852)).

What makes a small model robust is well established, if mostly outside our exact setting. Calibration fit on i.i.d. data falls short under shift, and errors run toward overconfidence. The temperature needed rises from 1.0–1.4 in-domain to 2.8–3.6 out of domain for BERT and RoBERTa ([Desai & Durrett](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07892)). Fitting the calibrator on deliberately perturbed validation data improves shift calibration while matching TS in-domain ([Tomani et al., CVPR 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10988)). Bounded maps cap worst-case confidence by construction. Our route floor caps maximum confidence near 0.95, so ECE under shift is bounded by (cap − accuracy) rather than (1 − accuracy). Keeping pretrained features intact helps shift. Linear-probe-then-fine-tune gives about 10% better OOD accuracy than full fine-tuning across 10 shift datasets ([LP-FT, ICLR 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10054)), though mostly in vision. Diverse training text helps. Task-adaptive MLM pretraining on unlabeled task text improves classification across 8 tasks ([Gururangan et al., ACL 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10964)). LLM-generated augmentation helps for clearly distinct intents but not near-synonyms ([Sahu et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.01959)), which suits five coarse queues. On abstention under shift, a small error calibrator trained on held-out and OOD data answered 56% of questions at 80% accuracy against 48% for MaxProb ([Kamath et al., ACL 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09462)). Density signals help when errors are epistemic (sparse or shifted inputs) but hurt in dense ambiguous regions. Rank-mixing them with softmax response gives 2–13% relative AUC-RC gains ([HUQ, ACL 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.652)). Grounded is precisely where errors would be epistemic on a new dataset, since it has zero in-domain errors to lose. The thing to avoid is entropy-minimising test-time adaptation. TENT cost XLM-R-large up to 12.6 EM on text shifts ([Ye et al., EMNLP Findings 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2302.04618)), and it sharpens wrong predictions.

There is one genuine dilemma with no free answer. If the judges' labels are *cleaner* than ours, our ε-floor and approve ceiling make Decide under-confident. On confident route bins that costs roughly 0.8ε ≈ 0.05 ECE, about −1 point. If their labels are equally noisy, the floor is exactly right. Proper-scoring-rule theory says that calibrating to the noisy posterior is correct when test labels share the noise process ([Gneiting & Raftery, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437)). Hanada & Futami (Oct 2026) show that uncorrected recalibration on noisy labels degrades calibration with respect to *clean* labels ([arXiv 2610.19543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19543)). The hedge is an ε somewhat below the out-of-fold estimate, chosen on the shifted set described next.

### How to build the shifted-dev stress set

The stress set has to answer one question: when the model is wrong on unfamiliar inputs, is it uncertain? Hold out half of it, sealed, for a final honest number, and use the other half to choose T, ε, thresholds, checkpoint and any interpolation weight. Report the dev score and the shifted score side by side. Hendrycks et al. note that IID accuracy "is not perfectly predictive of OOD accuracy" ([arXiv 2004.06100](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.06100)), and CheckList users found about 3× as many bugs as with held-out accuracy alone ([Ribeiro et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04118)).

| Component | Construction | What it measures | Size / hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leave-one-template-out | Mask numbers and entities, MinHash-cluster the 6,500 tickets by skeleton, GroupKFold by cluster (retraining is about 2 min per run) | Template memorisation on all three decisions | full train / 3 h |
| Register paraphrase | Local LLM rewrites 1,500 dev tickets into Bitext's variation axes: typos, colloquial, keyword-mode, long/rambling, negation, politeness, abbreviations ([Bitext card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/bitext/Bitext-customer-support-llm-chatbot-training-dataset)). Amounts, tiers and KB facts are locked; spot-check 100. | Lexical robustness of route and approve | 1,500 / 3 h |
| Approve format and cap | "$1,234.50", "1.2k", "USD 300", "300 bucks", "three hundred", €, ranges, multiple amounts (label invariant); cap ± ε (directional); unseen tier names; all caps ×1.5 (concept shift, expected to fail) | Parser brittleness; how wide the near-cap abstain band must be | 600 / 2 h |
| Grounded realism | Correct drafts containing ticket-specific numbers absent from the KB (order IDs, the customer's amount, dates); greetings and empathy lines; heavy paraphrase positives ("two weeks"/"14 days"); deleted-evidence and baseless-promise negatives; KB as bullets, long prose, distractor paragraphs | Whether the −8 veto and scope rule fire confidently on correct drafts | 600 / 3 h |
| Public grounding proxy | RAGTruth-QA (MIT) and LLM-AggreFact ClaimVerify/LFQA/ExpertQA, converted to (KB, draft) pairs ([RAGTruth](https://github.com/ParticleMedia/RAGTruth); [LLM-AggreFact](https://llm-aggrefact.github.io/)) | Distance from real RAG hallucinations; a TinyLettuce-17M comparison point | 1,000 / 2 h |
| Public route proxy | Bitext 27 intents (CDLA-Sharing-1.0) and BANKING77 (CC-BY-4.0) mapped onto the 5 queues; CLINC150 out-of-scope as abstain probes ([BANKING77](https://huggingface.co/datasets/PolyAI/banking77)) | Real phrasing; out-of-scope behaviour | 1,000 / 2 h |
| Prior and noise shift | Resample dev to other queue mixes and approve rates; relabel about 300 items by hand for a clean-label subset | Bias-term and ε sensitivity; the clean-vs-noisy dilemma above | 300 relabels / 3 h |

ExpertQA is near label-noise-limited for every model (59–61 BAcc, including GPT-4o), so a low score there is no signal ([LLM-AggreFact](https://llm-aggrefact.github.io/)). Mapping Bitext intents to queues and paraphrasing with an LLM both inherit the LLM's labelling errors on near-synonymous classes ([Sahu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.01959)), so filter with a second classifier. Total build time is about 18 hours for everything, or about 9 hours for the first four rows, which cover the failure modes that matter most.

The ordering reflects three judgements worth stating openly. Rows 2–5 come first because they are cheap and rest on artifacts or code we already have, and row 3 targets the single largest exposure. Row 2 is cheap because the 32M and 150M models and the four seeds already exist. Our measured ensemble result (84.01 for four seeds, 83.77 for the mean single seed, 84.78 for the shipped seed) looks like a loss only against a lucky seed. On a new dataset the expected comparison is ensemble against an average seed, which favours the ensemble by about 0.25, inside the noise. Only the shifted set can say whether that grows. Rows 6–7 are the expensive structural fixes. They are worth doing only if the stress set confirms that approve's parser and grounded's rules are the failure points. The in-domain items (11, 12, 16) together are worth well under a point and should not displace robustness work.

## A paper-level claim: noise ceilings, not model size, bound tiny decision models

The credible novel contribution is not a new architecture. Jev and Laya already own "typed questions, calibrated answers", and Ettin, MiniCheck and LettuceDetect already own the model side. Our contribution is **a measurement framework for decision APIs scored on noisy labels, with a 17M CPU model as the worked example**. It has three parts. (1) The ε-mixture calibrator is Bayes-correct for equally noisy evaluation and bounded under shift. It is forward correction ([Patrini et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03683)) applied post hoc, which TS alone cannot represent. This is the opposite target to the clean-label recalibration Hanada & Futami propose ([arXiv 2610.19543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19543)), and we can state when each one is right. (2) Normalised selective-risk metrics have a closed-form ceiling, `1 − η/e` under input-independent noise and `1 − 1.25η/e` when noise concentrates on easy cases. Our route head already sits on it at 0.536. So an "abstention gap" on such benchmarks is not model failure, and leaderboards should report headroom-normalised scores. The abstention notes found no published name for this metric family, so the novelty is "not found in our search", not proven. (3) Under shift, a hybrid symbolic-plus-17M model's score is dominated by whether its errors are confident, and bounded symbolic evidence preserves abstention where hard vetoes destroy it.

The experiment that backs it uses grounded as a clean laboratory, because it is the one task with no native label noise. Inject random flips into grounded labels at 0, 2, 5 and 10% in both train and evaluation. Fit five calibrators: TS, TS+bias, the ε-mixture, Dirichlet/Beta, and isotonic. Report ECE under three estimators (the scorer's 15-bin equal-width, equal-mass, smECE), Brier skill, and AbstScore against the predicted `1 − η/e` ceiling, with bootstrap CIs over five seeds. Then cross that grid with the shift axis from the stress set (in-domain, leave-one-template-out, grounded realism, RAGTruth-QA) and two rule variants (hard −8 veto vs bounded learnable flag). Add the 300-item hand-relabelled route subset to test the clean-vs-noisy calibration-target prediction on real noise. The headline figure is AbstScore against injected η, with the theoretical line overlaid, and the secondary figure is score under shift for hard and soft symbolic rules. Training runs take about 2 minutes each, so the full grid (about 5 η × 2 rule variants × 5 seeds) is roughly 2 GPU-hours plus 1–2 days of engineering. Part (1) rests on textbook theory (proper scoring rules, forward correction), so reviewers will judge the paper on parts (2) and (3), and those stand or fall on the measured fit to the ceiling line.

## Soups, smoothing, test-time adaptation and a dozen more will not help

Several ideas look like frontier upgrades but are measured failures for us or are contradicted by the evidence for this metric mix. **Seed ensembles and weight soups** are already measured. Four-seed logit averaging gave 84.01 against the shipped seed's 84.78, and soups dropped grounded to 96.2% raw accuracy ([context.md §7](../team/context.md)). Ensembles stay worth one re-check on the shifted set (row 2) but no more engineering. **Bigger encoders and distillation from big teachers** (Ettin 150M/400M, DeBERTa-v3-large, a Laya-initialised encoder) gave no in-domain gain, which is what noise-bound ceilings predict. **Dynamic int8** cost about 3 points through the outlier mechanism ([Bondarenko et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.12948)). It is worth revisiting only with static S8S8 QDQ and node exclusion, and only to afford a larger model. **Label smoothing** degrades selective classification ([Xia et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14715)). **Noisy-label learning** (co-teaching, DivideMix, GCE/SCE, cleanlab pruning) does not reliably help BERT at low random-noise rates ([Zhu et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2022.insights-1.8/)). Pruning our vague, near-uniformly labelled tickets would teach the model false confidence, and robust losses would break approve's aleatoric ceiling. **MC dropout** needs at least 20 passes to match softmax response in NLP ([Xin et al., 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.84.pdf)). **Learned selection heads** (SelectiveNet, Deep Gamblers, ConfidNet) lose to the classifier's own softmax ([Feng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.09034)). **Standalone Mahalanobis or energy scores** can fall well behind softmax response on ambiguous text ([HUQ](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.652)). **TENT-style test-time adaptation** is unreliable on text (up to −12.6 EM, [Ye et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2302.04618)). It assumes batch statistics that LayerNorm models serving single requests do not have.

The rest fail on our constraints rather than on evidence. **Isotonic regression and histogram binning** as final maps produce step functions that can game binned ECE but lose Brier resolution and do not extrapolate under shift ([Mix-n-Match](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07329) notes isotonic "can degrade accuracy" with small data). **Conformal prediction** gives coverage guarantees but changes neither p nor its ranking, so it adds nothing to ECE, Brier or the scorer's flag-blind abstention metric ([Angelopoulos & Bates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07511)). **Gradient-surgery multi-task optimisers** match tuned scalarisation at best ([Kurin et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.04122)). **Zero-shot classifiers or large grounding checkers in the hot path** fail on latency and fine-grained labels: GLiClass-edge scores 0.027 F1 on BANKING77 ([card](https://huggingface.co/knowledgator/gliclass-edge-v3.0)), HHEM takes about 1.5 s per 2k tokens on CPU ([card](https://huggingface.co/vectara/hallucination_evaluation_model)), and a second model breaks the one-shared-model rule anyway. **Early exit** trades accuracy for latency we do not need ([DeeBERT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.12993)). **RLCD-style proper-scoring-rule RL** is the Jev/Laya training story, but Laya still ships with mean ECE 0.466 before a temperature refit ([Laya card](https://huggingface.co/convaiinnovations/laya)). Nothing published shows it beating MLE plus post-hoc calibration. **New encoders** (UltraBERT, LFM2.5) start at 150M+ and have only author-reported results.

The pitch writes itself from the defensible rows. Decide is **about 25× smaller than Laya** (17M vs 421M). It runs **about 40–160× faster than Jev's measured round trips** on commodity CPU with no network, a gap that includes Jev's network time, which is why it exists. Its abstain signal halves the error rate on the retained 80%, where Laya's own card reports an escalate head with AUROC 0.30. Laya's numbers also make our strategic argument for us: its generalist checkpoint scores 0.362 zero-shot, below the 0.461 majority baseline, while its fine-tuned specialist reaches 0.766. In this category, specialisation beats generality, and that is our design.

Three claims must stay out of the pitch. First, we have no accuracy number on any benchmark Jev or Laya were measured on, so "beats Jev" is unsupported. Jev's 78.7 on LLM-AggreFact is above every small open checker, and we have not run ours. Second, Jev's "can't hallucinate" is a schema guarantee, as the TypeSafe post itself admits its hallucination figure "is not empirical". We should claim only the narrow, true version: outputs are schema-closed and abstention is explicit. Third, our ECE figures are in-domain. Until the shifted set exists, "calibrated" has to mean "calibrated on dev, with bounded maps that cap overconfidence". Two cheap additions would strengthen the pitch: an error-AUROC number comparable to Laya's 0.77 (row 17), and a RAGTruth-QA score alongside TinyLettuce-17M's 68.5 F1 on the same backbone.

## Conclusion

The survey inverts the usual question. Decide's in-domain score is bounded by the dataset, not the model: route abstention already sits on the flip-noise ceiling, approve is capped by hidden account history, and seed luck is as large as most remaining gains. So the frontier's model-side advances (bigger encoders, ensembles, distillation, fancier calibrators) have nowhere to show up on our dev set. What they can still do is change *how the system fails* on unfamiliar data. Because the judges' scorer charges confident errors in quality, ECE, Brier skill and abstention at once, the difference between confidently wrong and honestly uncertain is worth roughly ten points on grounded alone. That makes the most valuable next engineering hours the least glamorous ones. A stress set comes first, then turning hard rules into bounded evidence, a parser in place of memorised caps, and calibration fit on shifted data.

The same insight is the research contribution. Decision APIs evaluated on noisy labels need headroom-normalised metrics and calibration targets that match the label process. Tiny models are a feature of this regime, not a compromise, because when labels are the ceiling, the 17M model that knows when it is unsure is the state-of-the-art one.
